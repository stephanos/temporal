## Recovery representation and validation

`RecoveryImage::decode` no longer calls `node_for_disk_object` for persisted
paths or populates `fs.used_bytes` from their payloads. The existing
`validate_tree` pass returns its checked size so decode retains the checked
cross-volume total without constructing namespace nodes. `clone_durable`
continues to rebuild and bound the final namespace.

The consumer sweep found only `Storage::recover` as a production decoder
caller. It immediately calls `image.recover`, whose `clone_durable` reads
volume state, limits, configuration and inode counters. Re-encoding reads
volumes and filesystem counters, and does not need namespace nodes. Other
`image.fs` consumers are the existing image tests. Neither the removed nodes
nor their byte charge is consumed before recovery.

Authentication, framing, ordering, model/configuration checks, object and path
inode uniqueness, root-kind validation, operation/pending/store consistency,
inode headroom, fault occurrences and SQLite lock refusal remain in place.
Valid partial persistence can retain a child without its parent's path; its
existing authenticated fixture remains accepted. Complete evidence, refused
history and image limits retain their independent code paths and saturating
admission calculations.

The new focused test first failed with exit 101 at the assertion that decoded
volume namespace nodes were absent (`/tmp/fn183-red.log`). It now verifies
that decoding retains no durable namespace nodes or byte charge, recovery
restores the seven-byte payload and charge, and re-encoding is byte-identical.
The retained Go corpus additionally checks over 100 authenticated images,
history/orphans, repeated recovery, fault occurrences and inode continuation.
