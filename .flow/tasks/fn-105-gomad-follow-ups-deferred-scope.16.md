## Done summary
Investigation receipt on darwin/arm64 (2026-10-01, toolchain key 8d28bd44); nothing is fixed, the skip stays, and the correction is open work in fn-105. linux/amd64 was not run.

Cause: under clock_tick forward, gomadTimeNow adds 1 to 1024 ns to a cumulative offset on every time.Now and never subtracts, so time.Now led the timer clock by about 0.33 s (about 650,000 reads) when UpdateWhilePaused_AfterWindow_ExtendsDispatch polled. context.WithTimeout arms its timer for time.Until(time.Now()+d), which is d plus the lead, and each gRPC hop re-derives the deadline from grpc-timeout and adds the lead again. The frontend hop, the matching hop, and matching's WithDeadlineBuffer child add three leads; when they exceed the 1 s empty-response budget the client's 3 s deadline fires first. Probed trace, seed 5: client deadline 29.7180, matching child 29.7206, client first by 2.5 ms. The recorded "few microseconds" is the lead of a fresh process, not of the suite.

Reproduction: the recorded seed-11 timeout does not reproduce on the current toolchain (seed 11 passes with about 20 ms to spare, seed 17 with about 170 ms). The same failure reproduces on seeds 5, 12, 20, 21, 24 of 1 to 24, and a rerun of all seven seeds reproduced every stdout and stderr digest. Controls: under strict the subtest passes on all seven seeds with the child deadline exactly 1 s before the client's; natively it passes 3 of 3 with 999.8 ms between the deadlines; the leaf alone under forward passes with 989.5 ms. A standard-library fixture (fn105-d16-fixture-main.go.txt) exits 0 natively, under strict, and under forward after 0 and 600,000 reads, and exits 1 under forward after 700,000 reads on seeds 11 and 17.

Classification: gRPC relative-timeout propagation, Temporal's 1 s budget, and the test behave as they do natively. The README documents the per-deadline lag, but the offset has no cumulative bound, which fn-103 required. Owner: the Gomad runtime clock.

Proposed for a decision, not built or verified: one clock (add the draws to the virtual clock itself, as fn-103 specified). Its feasibility is not established, because fn-103's first implementation did this and broke the simulation time transport; reproducing that failure is the next action. Two partial fallbacks (offset-aware time.Since/time.Until; absorbing the offset on idle advances) and an accepted-limitation alternative are recorded with their residuals.
