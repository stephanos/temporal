# Capabilities and their laws

## Goal & Context
<!-- scope: business -->

This spec builds the vision's core requirement `### Reusable behavioral protocols (#PROTOCOLS)` (`.plans/UMPIRE4_VISION.md`): an entity declares the capabilities it has, binds each capability's parameters to its own vocabulary, and receives the laws of each capability and of each pair of capabilities as lifted Properties, Queries and generated Cases without listing them. Each law says what it does not promise; an entity that differs opts out or overrides with a recorded reason; a law is part of a protocol only once two entities adopt it.

Two existing claims motivate it, because neither fundamentally belongs to one feature. `terminalIsFinal` ("once an activity is over, no step changes its phase") is written in the standalone activity, again in the Nexus caller, and three more times as `terminalStays` on the admission record; it is one law, `terminalStatesAreFinal`, parameterized by a status type and its terminal set and phrased `once(terminal).keeps(status)`. `pausedIsNotDispatched` ("a paused activity is dispatched to no worker") is written on the activity product and three times as `notAdmittedWhilePaused` on the admission designs; it is the interaction law of two capabilities, `Pausable x Pollable`, and must apply to any entity that has both without an author remembering it. After the pilot neither claim is written in the standalone activity's own files: the activity declares capabilities and bindings, and the generated claims carry `<machine>.<law>` names with the baseline proving behavior unchanged.

The pilot is Terminate and Pause on the standalone activity (product, protocol and admission designs, both composition families) and a new minimal Nexus-operation Model grounded in `chasm/lib/nexusoperation` as the second entity; Pausable on the fn-119 workflow example is a later gated task. The inventory task classifies every existing claim as a single-capability law, an interaction law or genuinely feature-specific, so the catalog admits only laws with two instantiating entities and what the entity does differently stays in its feature.

It serves the feature developer who writes the next entity and gets its universal checks for free, and the reviewer who reads a law once with its server citation instead of five restatements. Design study and inventory: `.plans/SEMANTIC_PROTOCOLS.md`; the claim patterns the laws are written with: `.plans/TEMPORAL_PATTERNS.md`.

## Architecture & Data Models
<!-- scope: technical -->

Three things, none of which needs an IR schema field.

**A capability** is a typed declaration on a machine that binds a protocol's parameters to the machine's own vocabulary: status predicates (`terminal`, `paused`, `running`), actions (`terminate = control(Control.terminate)`), the rejecting outcome, a status table for Describable, how to reach a live state (`reach`) for the Scenarios functional laws need, and the Limits generated `verify` Queries run under. A capability is not a machine; the entity's machine stays the only machine, and the laws are read on it. A machine declares at most one `refines`, which the protocol already spends on the product, so a capability cannot be an opaque machine. The capability whose work is handed out by polling is `Pollable` (the owner's name; `.plans/SEMANTIC_PROTOCOLS.md` calls it `Dispatchable`, which captures nothing more: its two fields are the dispatching action and the running predicate).

**A law** is an ordinary top-level Scala `def` in `temporal/laws` (Temporal kit) or `umpire/laws` (entity-neutral) that takes the model and the capability's fields and returns a `Property` of that model, fn-112 R4's shape, written with fn-112's claim patterns where one fits; the lifter lifts it through the same fold as every def-built declaration, binding the function-valued arguments at lift time. Four forms, each lowering to IR that exists:

| Law form | Lowers to |
| --- | --- |
| single capability, one machine | transition or same-step Property on that machine, plus a `verify` over the machine's free Scenario from its start under the declared Limits |
| interaction of two capabilities of one machine | the same, over both capabilities' parameters |
| interaction across two entities | a Property of a composition, `after.records(_.member, fact)` |
| functional (a `find` with a Scenario) | a Scenario from `reach ++ Seq(action)`, a `when action holds` Property, an `.expect` |

A law carries a `Law` value (name, server citation, `promises`, `doesNotPromise`) as plain data beside the def, not a docstring the compiler would have to retain; the generated table view prints it, so an entity can hold one law and waive another.

**The catalog** is a Scala value: `umpire/laws` defines the `Catalog` type and the entity-neutral entries, keyed by capability type and unordered pair and naming law defs by reference; `temporal/laws` provides the one `given Catalog` that adds the Temporal laws, and `capabilities(m)(…)` takes it as a given. The lifter folds the given's value like a `Vector` of declarations, so the framework and the lifter name nothing of Temporal. Pairs are found from the declared set, never listed by the author. **An instantiating entity is a machine, with its own state type, that declares the capability**; a composition reading a member's capability through its projection does not count again, and neither does a derived machine. A law enters the catalog only with two instantiating entities among the checked-in Models, and the catalog test (task 1), the lifter and lint (task 5) all count by that one definition. A capability declared on the product is read on the protocol through the refinement the protocol declares (fn-112 R8); a law that needs protocol-only vocabulary, or a realization (functional laws), is declared on the protocol's own capability.

**Opt-out and override, with a recorded reason.** `except(law, because = …)` lifts no Property and no Query for that law; `overriding(law -> ownDef, because = …)` lifts the entity's own def under the law's name. Both are refused without a reason.

**The law sidecar.** The IR has no description field on a Property or Function and this spec adds none, so the lifter writes, beside each IR file, one sidecar `model/ir/<file>.laws.json` listing for that file every generated `<machine>.<law>` with its law, citation, `promises`, `doesNotPromise`, the capability bindings it was expanded from, every `except`/`overriding` with its reason and position, and the catalog's law list with each law's instantiating machines. Lint (task 5) and the table view (task 6) read it; fn-120 R7's accepted-findings file receives the waivers from it. It is a checked-in artifact of the gate like the IR, not IR.

**Generated names.** A Property's Definition ID is family plus name, so generated claims are named `<machine>.<law>` deterministically; the catalog expansion registers the law's Property under that name regardless of the string the law's body writes, so a direct call of the same def (fn-112's authored instances) keeps its written name. Authored declarations keep their `val` names (fn-112 R7); the lifter never requires a `val`. Generated claims are additions; the authored claims they replace are retired by name, and every ID change is an allow-listed entry in `tools/umpire/internal/golden/original.json`, never silent.

**Laws pin cells; they never add rows.** In the per-operation modality table fn-120.3's lint and fn-120.4's explorer derive from the IR (`.plans/MODALITIES.md`), a law is rendered as the modality it pins on the cells of a capability's action: `Pausable x Pollable` gives the `paused -> MUST NOT` cell of the dispatching action (`pausedIsNotDispatched`), `Closable` the `terminal -> MUST NOT` cell (`closedIsRejectedUniformly`). The table's rows come from the step function alone; a law never adds, removes or rewrites a row, and the view lists the cells of a capability's action that no law pins.

**Against the task-queue entity (fn-112 R20).** The queue is a reusable entity (an opaque contract machine providers refine); a capability is not. The two meet in cross-entity interaction laws, which are composition Properties the queue entity and the feature share, written through `queueLaws(m)`.

**Core and sugar (fn-112's file rule).** `capabilities`, the capability types, `except`, `overriding`, the `Catalog` type and the law defs are core: they introduce meaning the IR and the sidecar need. Any convenience spelling this spec adds (an infix or abbreviated form of a declaration, a helper whose meaning a core form already expresses) lives in `model/umpire/Syntax.scala` or `model/temporal/laws/Syntax.scala`, documented with the core form it stands for, with its lifter matching in `model/irgen/Syntax.scala` and a fixture proving IR equality with the core spelling; core files import nothing from sugar files.

## API Contracts
<!-- scope: technical -->

The author surface. Names are the contract; a task may change a name only by recording the reason in its done summary.

```scala
val productCapabilities = capabilities(activityProduct, limits = five)(          // laws read on the product, and on the protocol through its refinement
  Closable(status = Product.status, terminal = Product.terminal, rejected = Outcome.notFound),
  Pausable(pause = control(Control.pause), unpause = control(Control.unpause), paused = Product.paused),
  Pollable(dispatch = attemptStart, running = Product.running))
val protocolCapabilities = capabilities(activityProtocol, limits = six)(         // functional laws need the realization, which is the protocol's
  Terminable(terminate = control(Control.terminate), settled = ProtocolFact.statusTerminated, reach = Seq(start())),
  Cancelable(requestCancel = control(Control.requestCancel), requested = ProtocolFact.statusCancelRequested, reach = Seq(start())),
  Describable(status = activityStatus))                                           // the fact-to-status table fn-112.9 declares once
capabilities(schedule)(Terminable(…)).except(recordsReasonAndIdentity, because = "…")
capabilities(nexusOperation)(Closable(…)).overriding(closedIsRejectedUniformly -> NexusOperation.closedAnswersFailedPrecondition, because = "…")
```

Every field of a capability that takes a function is a reference to a named def of the lifted sources (`Product.terminal`), never a lambda literal; the lifter binds it at lift time (fn-112.4 binds such arguments for direct def calls; this spec extends the binding to a capability's fields). A `keeps` projection may be such a bound def when its body is a field path.

A law, by signature only (the shape fn-112 R4 gives its three shared claims):

```scala
/** No row leaves the terminal set. Law(cites = "chasm/lib/activity/handler.go", promises = …, doesNotPromise = …). */
def terminalStatesAreFinal[S, P](m: Declares[S])(status: S => P, terminal: P => Boolean): Property[S]   // once(s => terminal(status(s))).keeps(status)
def pausedIsNotDispatched[S](m: Declares[S])(paused: S => Boolean, running: S => Boolean): Property[S]  // never(s => running(s.state)).from(paused)
```

**What the lifter emits** for `capabilities(m, limits)(…)`: for each law the catalog brings, one Property of `m` named `<m>.<law>` (the law's body with the fields bound) and one Query named `<m>.<law>` (`verify` over `m`'s free Scenario from its declared start under `limits`, or `find` over `reach ++ action` with an `.expect`), each with a `total` the lifter computes from the formula fn-112 R19 states; and the sidecar entry. The emitted IR uses only the Property, Scenario, Query and Function records that exist.

**Retirement and answers.** A retired `verify` Query is reproduced by the generated one when the generated verdict (holds or violated) equals the retired one; a generated Query that reaches its limit where the retired one held is a finding in the done summary, fixed by raising the declaration's `limits`, never by keeping the authored Query. A generated find Query replaces an authored one only when its Case equals the authored Case byte-for-byte apart from the Query name, recorded as a rename.

**Lint (R7)**, by example; final kind names follow fn-120.3's conventions:

```text
activityProduct  law-waived-without-reason   except(recordsReasonAndIdentity) names no reason      Model.scala:88
schedule         reason-names-no-law         because "…" names a law the catalog does not bring    Model.scala:91
nexusOperation   parameter-without-citation  rejected = failedPrecondition cites no server code    Model.scala:40
catalog          law-with-one-instance       cancelIsRequested is instantiated by one machine       laws/Catalog.scala:22
```

## Edge Cases & Constraints
<!-- scope: technical -->

- **Behavior is frozen for what exists.** Tables, Definition IDs, refinement rows, fingerprints and Query answers of authored declarations, and the bytes of every existing Case, equal fn-112's task-1 baseline. The baseline harness (`tools/umpire/internal/golden/original.go`, allow-list `original.json`) treats the IR and Case sets as closed, so this spec adds exactly three delta categories, each an allow-listed entry by name and never a waived fingerprint: a *retired* Property or Query with the generated `<machine>.<law>` twin that replaces it and the twin's verdict; a *new Case* named by its generated Query; a *new IR file* for a new Model. Task 3 lands all three; task 4 uses them.
- **No IR schema change.** `Query.total` is computed by the lifter for generated Queries from the formula fn-112 R19 states, and Go validates it as for any Query. Law text travels in the sidecar. A transition Property with `when` stays unsupported and every law avoids it; a law that needs one is a finding for a reader spec, not a workaround here.
- **The lifter refuses what it cannot read, at its line.** A capability whose action the machine does not bind; a predicate of another state type (compiler, by the capability's type parameters); a lambda literal in a capability field; an `except` of a law the catalog does not bring; an `overriding` whose def has another signature; a missing reason; two capabilities of one kind on one machine; a `capabilities` declaration with no `limits`.
- **A Model that breaks a law is rejected naming the law and the binding** (the vision's acceptance test): the generated `verify` Query's violation names `<machine>.<law>` and the gate's report adds the capability binding from the sidecar.
- **Wrong universals surface, they do not hide.** `recordsReasonAndIdentity` is already false for the schedule (`chasm/lib/scheduler` records `Closed = true` only). Opt-out with a reason is how a wrong universal is recorded; parameters (`rejected`, pause of held work) are where the server differs on purpose, and each parameter value cites the server code that backs it.
- **Reader cost.** A free-Scenario `verify` at depth 5 and above is the expensive Query today. A generated Query's bound is the declaration's `limits`, and its `total` is checked as for any Query.
- **Sequencing and file overlap.** This spec starts after fn-112.10 (constructs, DefinitionScope, the structural Case-byte freeze) and runs alongside fn-114, which rolls fn-112's constructs out to the Nexus caller, its close policy and the worker. The files they edit are disjoint except the ones listed here: this spec adds `model/umpire/laws`, `model/temporal/laws`, `model/temporal/features/nexusoperation`, `model/umpire/Capabilities.scala` and `model/irgen/Capabilities.scala` with one dispatch case in the lifter's claim fold; fn-114 owns `model/temporal/features/nexuscaller/**`, `model/temporal/shared/worker/**` and the retirement of string-named forms. Where both touch one file (the lifter's claim fold, the activity's `Properties.scala` files, the gate's root list or the activity's IR-file declaration, `tools/umpire/internal/golden/{config,original}.json`), whichever lands second rebases, and neither edits the other's cases. The Nexus-operation Model is written in the captured-name forms from the start so fn-114.7 need not touch it. The activity rollout registers the `capabilities` values in the activity's Scala IR-file declaration when fn-114.1 has landed and in `model/gate/Roots.scala` otherwise, and fn-114.1 carries them over.

| Phase handoff | Completion gate | Next work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constructs and Case freeze | fn-112.10 done | task 1 (laws, catalog, inventory) may start; fn-114.1 may start in parallel |
| Lint and accepted findings | fn-120.3 done (after fn-114 closes) | task 5 (lint kinds, accepted-findings integration) |
| Derived awaits | fn-118 closed | generated Cases take their wait bounds from hints; until then from the kit's one interval |
| Workflow example | fn-119.4 done | task 7 (Pausable on the workflow) |

- **Gates.** Each task runs the scoped parts of the model gate, `make lint-model` and the Go tests of the Umpire tooling. The closing task runs all three in full and `make lint-code-fast`.

## Boundaries
<!-- scope: business -->

- No IR schema change; law text travels in the sidecar beside the IR.
- No capability as a machine; no second refinement per machine.
- A law pins rows of the table; it never adds or removes one. Rows come from the step function.
- No `Deadlined` or `Retryable` capability in the pilot, and no law on Reset, Poll-result, Update or Signal; `recordedStays` and the Nexus caller's capabilities are candidates once a second entity needs them.
- No change to the Go reader's Property semantics; a law that needs a transition Property with `when` is a finding for a reader spec.
- No change to existing Case bytes; no retirement of an authored find Query whose Case a generated one does not reproduce byte-for-byte.
- No law with a single instantiating machine; `atMostOneActive` and `startedByPollingWorker` stay authored in this spec.
- No table-driven settlement law (`completes`, `syncSucceeds`, `completionSucceeds` stay the feature's own content).
- No derived wait bounds: generated Cases take their waiting from the kit, and from fn-118's hints once fn-118 lands.

## Decision Context
<!-- scope: both -->

- **Laws are defs that return a Property**, fn-112 R4's shape, not predicates and not expression templates in the lifter: a predicate-returning def cannot be written with the claim patterns (they are `PropertyBuilder` methods) and the lifter's `stepFunction` accepts a lambda or an eta-expanded def, not a call returning a closure. The lifter owns only the catalog expansion, the Scenario and Query it adds, and the sidecar.
- **`Pollable`, not `Dispatchable`.** The owner's name for the capability whose work is handed out by polling; `.plans/SEMANTIC_PROTOCOLS.md`'s `Dispatchable` captures nothing `Pollable` cannot (its fields are the dispatching action and the running predicate), so one name is used.
- **One definition of "instantiating entity"**: a machine with its own state type that declares the capability. It is what makes `Pausable x Pollable` a law today (the product and the admission record are two machines) and what keeps `atMostOneActive` (only the record has `active`) and `startedByPollingWorker` (its second instance, `repliedByPollingWorker`, is in the Nexus caller, which fn-114 owns and which declares no capability here) authored until a second machine declares them.
- **Pairs are found, not listed**, because the owner's point is that `Pausable x Pollable` must apply without an author remembering it.
- **Opt-out needs a reason** because a wrong universal is the main risk and the schedule already shows one.
- **Generated names are `<machine>.<law>`** because a Property's Definition ID is family plus name, and two machines of one family must not instantiate one law under one name; it also keeps fn-112 R1's frozen IDs untouched, since generated claims are additions and retirements are allow-listed by name in `original.json`.
- **The catalog is a Scala given, not a lifter table**, so the lifter names nothing of Temporal and the Temporal laws register from `temporal/laws`; the lifter folds it as data.
- **Law text travels in a sidecar** because the IR has no text field on a Property or Function and Boundaries forbid adding one; a sidecar beside the IR is reviewable and read by lint and the table view, the same shape fn-120 chose for accepted findings.
- **Functional-law capabilities sit on the protocol** because a find Query lowers to a Case only through a Realization, which is per machine, and the activity's is the protocol's; Closable, Pausable and Pollable sit on the product and are read on the protocol through its refinement.
- **The activity declares `Cancelable`** although the pilot's named operations are Terminate and Pause: the Cancelable laws need two machines, the activity already claims `cancelRequestedWhileStarted`, and the Nexus-operation Model's `cancelIsRequested` would otherwise be a feature claim with a longer name.
- **Function-argument binding is fn-112's.** fn-112.4 binds a function-valued argument that references a lifted top-level def, so its R4 defs can take `terminal`; this spec reuses that and adds only the path through a capability's fields.
- **`Describable` is a table fn-112.9 declares.** The activity realization's `awaitStatus(fact, enumValue)` pairs become one fact-to-status table there; this spec reads it and adds the derived await to generated Cases, not the performs.
- **Rejected as overkill:** a capability as an opaque machine (a machine has one `refines`); a general multi-entity catalog DSL (the catalog is one value); auto-retirement of authored claims (retirement is by name, per task, against the harness); an inert text field on Property (a schema change for prose).
- **Why alongside fn-114 and not after it.** The two edit disjoint files apart from the listed shared ones; waiting would serialize about eight tasks behind a rollout this spec does not need, since the Nexus caller gets its capabilities only after this pilot proves the shape.
- **Modal refinement (#ZOOM) is deferred.** A product law read on the protocol through its refinement is a MUST the refinement does not by itself preserve; the two checks that would (a product row no protocol row carries; a product progress claim read through the map) are recorded in fn-120's Decision Context for a later spec, and this spec's table view marks a product law the protocol has not been checked against as inherited.
- **Plan-review amendment (2026-10-03).** The first review found the two-entity rule encoded three ways, no data path for law text, a harness that is closed to retirements and new files, a law shape that could not be written with the patterns, two catalog locations, no Limits for generated verifies, and a product-side realization gap. Each is settled above: one entity definition, the sidecar, three allow-listed delta categories in task 3, Property-returning laws, the catalog as a given, `limits` on the declaration, and functional laws on the protocol. Task 4 now depends on task 3.
