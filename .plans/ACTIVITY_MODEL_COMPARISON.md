# Standalone activity: Umpire's Scala model vs chasm/lib/activity/model

Research note, 2026-10-05 (claude-fable-5-1, read-only). Compares upstream's Go behaviour model (#12044, #12081) with the Umpire standalone activity as it stands after fn-126.4 and as projected by fn-126's pending decisions.

**Verdict.** On the behaviour both models cover (poll, respond, pause/unpause, cancel, terminate, the three deadlines, backoff), B is already shorter per behaviour, clearer to navigate and far safer: it is a finite, exhaustively tabled, refinement-checked, law-checked, lint-audited Model that Go, Quint and P agree on and that explains live Runs, where A is 606 code lines of hand-written Go switch statements checked by 11 smoke tests and ~65 hand-written traces (`chasm/lib/activity/model/model.go`, `tests/activity_parity_test.go`). But A is more *comprehensive* on the activity's own semantics, and in three places it is more *precise*: B has no retry policy (unlimited retries, no retry of timeouts), no reset, no heartbeat, no start delay, no by-ID responds, and no error kinds other than NotFound; and B's `backingOff` *phase* (rather than an orthogonal dispatchability field) makes B permit two things the server forbids: an unpause after a pause during backoff dispatches at once, and schedule-to-start can fire during backoff (`StandaloneActivity.scala:464,477` vs `model.go:239-244,312-322`). B also accepts a repeat RequestCancel that the server rejects (`:466-469` vs `model.go:201-202`). B's remaining length is mostly things A does not have at all (product level, refinement, Properties, Queries, realization, the history record and task-queue level); its remaining ceremony is ~65 lines, about half of which fn-126's pending decisions remove. Recommendation in one line: finish fn-126 as planned, then spend one spec on an orthogonal `dispatch` field, a retry policy and rejecting rows; those three close most of the precision and coverage gap at roughly +80 lines.

Status of (B): "current" = `umpire` branch after fn-126.4 (machine objects, `states`/`effects`/`rules`/`properties`/`implements`/`queries`, `init`, `type State`) plus lane-a's fn-126.5 markers (`NegativeControl`, `FailureModel` in `wt/lane-a/model/umpire/Markers.scala`). "Projected" = tasks 6-8 (`.flow/specs/fn-126-…md` decisions 16, 22-26 and R18-R20): `product/`/`system/` folders, fully qualified IDs with no pins or families, `Actor` for `Party`, no `val all`, no `extends Section`, `worker.poll`/`respond`, `ActivitySystem`/`ActivityRecord`, R20 structure lint. All quotations below are current code unless marked *projected*.

## 1. Shorter

**Mapping A → B.** `Poll` → `worker.attemptStart` (`poll` *projected*); `RespondCompleted/Failed/Canceled` → `worker.attemptResult(completed | failed(r) | canceled)` (`:155-162`; the `ByID` forms, `Failure.Type`, `HasHeartbeatDetails`, `LargeMessage` have no counterpart); `RequestCancel/Terminate/Pause/Unpause` → `caller.control(Control.*)` (`:137-144`); `ScheduleToStart/ScheduleToClose/StartToCloseElapses` → `deadline.*` guarded by the start's `Timeout` input (`:474-482`); `BackoffElapses` → `timers.backoff` (`:472`); `Initial(cfg)` → `init` + `in(unstarted)(caller.start ~> effects.schedule)` (`:320,437`); `handleEventInTerminalState` → `when(terminal)(caller.control ~> effects.notFound)` plus disabled worker/timer pairs (`:461`); `ImpossibleTimeWindowElapse` → a timer rule whose guard is false; `Reject NotFound` → `Outcome.notFound`. No counterpart: `Heartbeat`, `HeartbeatElapses`, `StartDelayElapses`, `Reset(KeepPaused, ResetHeartbeat)`, `UpdateOptions`, `ResetRequested`, `applyDeferredReset`, `MaxAttempts`, `NonRetryableTimeouts`, `RetryOutlivesScheduleToClose`.

**Counts for the shared behaviour.** A: 19 transition functions + 4 helpers, 370 code lines in `model.go`, plus 236 in `vocabulary.go` of which 132 are `String()` switches. B: Product + System rules 79 lines for 32 bindings under 26 headings (`:255-286,436-482`), effects 96 lines for 27 defs (`:223-253,370-434`), `states` 41, refinement 20, types 63, signature 77: ≈376 lines, of which ~110 are comments. Adding A's missing behaviour in B's terms costs roughly: reset +25, heartbeat +8, retry policy +12, start delay (with recommendation P1-1) +10, by-ID +8 ≈ +65 lines, still under A's 606. B pays nothing for enum printing, dispatch (`Transition`'s switch, `model.go:44-89`) or "unreachable" panics (`:120,152,…`, nine of them).

**Where B is longer it mostly says more**: Properties 60 lines (`:488-547`), `implements` 30 (`:296-309,559-574`), Queries 96 (`:582-677`), the composition with the worker 37, `exports` 42, `Realization.scala` 340 — A's equivalents are the 2,975 harness lines. `record/` + `withTaskQueue/` (773 lines) model behaviour A has none of.

**B's remaining ceremony, per line** (feature file): pins and families `:30,34-42` (6; *projected* away by decision 23); `extends Section` ×10 `:198,223,296,330,370,488,559,582,698,704` (*projected* away, 25); `val all =` for capabilities `:297,560` and the Query vector `:644-653` (12; decision 24); `type ProductStep/ProtocolStep` `:74,104` and the explicit `List[ProductStep]` on `notFound/keep/retry` `:241,405,428,431` (6; a `reject(outcome, s)` helper would remove them); `@unused s` `:375`; `.schema[…]`/`.example`/`.results` lowering metadata `:133,140-144,153,158-162` (12); `limits three total 864` hand-computed totals ×12 (`README.md:1044`); `.expect(satisfied | inconclusive(…))` ×6 (honest, but Known Gaps as Query metadata); `scenario("completed")` name strings ×8 kept for IR stability (R13); enum-qualification forced by name clashes (`ProductPhase.paused`, `Phase.unstarted`, `ProtocolFact.attemptCount` `:207,243,320,355-365,390,399`, ~15); positional state literals `:320,500-507`; `own(…)`/`synced(…)` wrappers and the stated `.starts(…)` in the composition Scenario `:714-721` (7, the `.starts` only because the golden compares positions, `:708-710`, gone with fn-124.7); `val entity = activity` ×2; `val evidence` `:324-327`; the 18-line header explaining the reading order (unneeded once R20's template exists). ## 2. Clearer

**"What happens when X in state Y."** In A, find the function for X and read its `switch s.Status` — but first check `Transition` (`model.go:48-50`) because terminal states are handled elsewhere, and `cfg` conditions sit inside the elapse functions (`:316,329,344,354`). There is no per-state view: "what can happen in Paused" means scanning all 19 functions. In B, grep `in(paused)`/`states.paused` in `rules` (`:461-482`), or read the lint's printed table per machine (`tools/umpire/lint` `WriteTables`, `UMPIRE_MODULES.md:45`), or the Quint module; the product machine gives the caller's view in 9 states. B's `because` strings (`:400,415`) are A's inline comments, but they survive into the table view.

**Vocabulary.** A uses Temporal's own words (RespondActivityTaskCompleted, PauseRequested, Dispatchability) and four model words (`Outcome`, `Reject`, `Possible`, `FindsTask`); a server engineer reads it cold. B asks for a larger vocabulary (actor, action, class, effect, rule, fact, outcome, Property, Scenario, Query, Limits, evidence, realization, capability, law, refinement, sync, Derived, Composition, NegativeControl, FailureModel), documented across `README.md` (1,262 lines) and `SEMANTICS.md` (880). fn-126's renames help (`poll`, `respond`, `ActivitySystem`, `ActivityRecord`), but B remains a language, A a file.

**Guards, outcomes, errors.** A's `Reject ErrorKind` puts the server's answer in the model, so #12081 was one model edit (`model.go:401-406`, FailedPrecondition → NotFound) and the harness checked it on both surfaces. A's `ImpossibleTimeWindowElapse` names a concept B expresses cleanly as a guard that never holds. B's `disabled`/absent rule is silence: the seven pause/unpause/cancel pairs the server answers FailedPrecondition are prose (`:454-460`) and lint acceptances, not rows, so a change of error kind on those pairs is invisible to B. `notFound` is the only answer B states, through `Closable(rejected = cited(Outcome.notFound, …))` (`:298-302`).

**A's "note to authors/AIs"** (`model.go:14-17`: update the model independently of the implementation; an AI whose change breaks conformance asks its operator) is a social rule with no enforcement. B's equivalents are mechanical: the gate requires `model/ir` and `model/cases` to equal a fresh lift, lint findings must be accepted with a reason, and the behaviour freeze is recorded per pair in `activity.lint.json`. B lacks A's one-line statement of intent; worth adopting as a header convention.

**Audience.** A: server engineers and assistants working in `chasm/lib/activity`, plus the parity harness authors. B: feature developers who read one file top to bottom (fn-126's goal) and the toolchain; a reader needs Scala 3 and the DSL.

## 3. Safer

**A guarantees** little statically: Go checks types; totality is `default: panic` on each switch, so an unhandled (status, event) pair surfaces only when a trace drives it; there is no disjointness problem (switches) but also no cross-function consistency check (the Cancel > Reset > Pause precedence is a comment, `:156-158`). Dynamically: `model_test.go` has 11 smoke tests and a 20-row `Possible` table; conformance is `driveActivityEvent` (`tests/activity_driver.go:367-385`): per event, `advance` refuses unrealizable events (`:238-255`), the RPC's error kind must equal `Outcome.Reject` (`:440-447`), and the server's `(closed, runState, attempt)` must equal `Outcome.Next` (`:396-409`). About 65 hand-written traces (`[]model.Event{` ×50 in `activity_parity_test.go`, ×15 in the metrics test) run against SAA and WFA alike. Not property-based, not exhaustive, no exploration; everything else (terminal status, retry interval, heartbeat details, truncation) is asserted ad hoc beside the model.

**B guarantees**, currently: every pair of every finite state and action class has a row or is disabled (`SEMANTICS.md:130-141`); rules of one class are disjoint over every state (`Machine.scala` `overlap`, `README.md:582-588`); every System row is carried by a Product row or is a stutter (`SEMANTICS.md:144-156`); laws `terminalStatesAreFinal`, `closedIsRejectedUniformly`, `pausedIsNotDispatched`, `terminateSettles`, `cancelIsRequested` as bounded `verify` Queries (`model/temporal/capabilities/*.scala`); declaration-order and placement lint (`README.md:846-880`); lint for `never-enabled`, `silent-rejection`, `unconstrained-result`, `witness-only`, `unread-refinement`, `unmodeled-api-value` (`activity.lint.json`); witnesses replayed through a second interpretation; Quint agreement on every reachable transition and Property and P on a monitor; 10 Cases run live with Model assessment; negative controls (`StaleAdmission`, the forgetful/volatile/lossy queues) prove the checks refuse a wrong design and failure models (`AdmissionResponseLoss`, `HeldAdmission`) prove the promise survives a fault (lane-a `Record.scala:351-355,446-448`). *Projected*: R20 structure lint, marker lint, FQN IDs.

**Bug classes.** A catches a wrong error kind on any driven pair, a wrong run state or attempt count after any driven event, a timer that fires when it should not (`awaitActivityTimeout`), and WFA/SAA divergence. A misses whatever no trace drives (reset-during-backoff is a model test, not a server test), internal model contradictions, and every race (A has no held dispatch, stale delivery or lost ack). B catches contradictions between levels (a System row the Product forbids), law breaks (a row from `paused` to `running`; a closed activity answering anything but `notFound` — #12081's change would have been a `closedIsRejectedUniformly` violation had B stated the FailedPrecondition rows), overlapping rules, admission races (`record/`), and a live Run whose evidence the Model cannot explain. B misses: FailedPrecondition/InvalidArgument answers (silent), the attempt count except where a Query pins it (`retryCompletes`, `:513-516`), workerStop and backoff steps (`keep`, `retry` are Known Gaps, `:427-431`), and the three over-permissions named in the verdict, which no Case drives — B's lint flags `backoff` and `attemptResult-failed-true` as `unconstrained-result`, which is the closest it comes.

## 5. Ideas to adopt

**From A:** (1) the authors/AIs note as a header convention and an `AGENTS.md` rule; (2) rejecting outcomes as table rows, so the error kind is checked, not prose; (3) an orthogonal dispatchability field; (4) `modelConfig()` deriving the model's view from the driver's config (`activity_driver.go:156-173`) — B's start inputs already do this, but B should also derive `attempts`/`retriesRemaining` from one input; (5) the "nominal vs real time window" comment (`model.go:300-310`) as `because` text on the deadline rules; (6) A's precedence note as a Property (`cancelIsNotUndone`: from `cancelRequested` no pause/reset leaves it); (7) per-event state assertions — B could add an `attemptCount` observation to every Case rather than only the retry Query; (8) a driver self-test that the model refuses unrealizable traces (`activity_driver_test.go:59-99`) — B's lowering refuses them, but a fixture stating it would document that.

**From B that A lacks:** the finite table and exhaustiveness; disjointness; the Product/System refinement; shared laws across entities (`Closable` on the activity and the Nexus operation); the lint of silent pairs and unconstrained results; negative controls and failure models; the record and queue levels with races; Quint/P cross-checks; Cases generated from witnesses; Model assessment of Runs; the structure lint; the single source for IDs and names. One strategic idea: `tools/umpire/model`'s interpreter could serve as A's `activityModel` (`activity_driver.go:226-255`) from `model/ir/activity.json`, so one Model drives both harnesses.

## 6. Recommendations

**P1 — precision and the three divergences (one follow-up spec after fn-126 closes; each changes tables, so it conflicts with R5's freeze and fn-126's "no semantic change" and must not land inside it).**
1. Orthogonal `dispatch` field replacing the `backingOff` phase; `startDelay` input and timer. Fixes unpause-after-backoff and schedule-to-start-in-backoff; enables start delay. Cost: medium (state type, refinement map, ~10 rule edits, IR/Cases regeneration).
2. Retry policy: `maxAttempts` start input, `states.retriesRemaining`, retryable timeouts (`startToClose`, later `heartbeat`) by two guarded rules. Cost: medium (~15 lines; state ×3).
3. Rejecting rows: `Outcome.failedPrecondition`, `invalidArgument`; replace the seven `silent-rejection` acceptances with rows; `closedIsRejectedUniformly` already takes `rejected` as a parameter. The lint acceptance itself says it waits for "a spec that lifts the behavior freeze". Cost: small-medium (Product must carry the same outcomes; no Case changes).
4. Repeat `RequestCancel` in `cancelRequested`: disable now (one row, one acceptance) or reject with (3). Cost: tiny.

**P2 — coverage and checks.**
5. Reset (`resetRequested`, `keepPaused`, deferred apply). Cost: medium-large (state ×2, ~25 lines, several Properties).
6. Heartbeat action and timeout. Cost: small.
7. By-ID responds as a `service` actor (decision 26 makes this natural). Cost: small-medium.
8. Declare `visible` on `ActivitySystem.refinement` so stutter facts are checked: `requestPause` records `statusPaused` while refining to a product stutter in `started` (`:358,413-415`; `SEMANTICS.md:148-152`). Cost: small; may surface a refinement rejection to decide.
9. Adopt A's header note and the precedence Property. Cost: tiny.
10. Ceremony not covered by fn-126: a `reject(outcome, s)` helper (drops the `Step` aliases), gate-computed `total` with the author's value optional, `scenario` names from the enclosing val, named-argument state literals, `entity` inferred from the actions' `.on(…)`. Cost: small Scala + small Go each; no conflict with recorded decisions (decision 4 already retired step-name ceremony in the same spirit).

**P3 — reach.**
11. A WFA realization of `ActivitySystem` (A's `wfaDriver`), giving B parity checking. Cost: large.
12. One Model for both harnesses: drive `tests/activity_driver.go` from `model/ir/activity.json` through the Go interpreter. Cost: large; strategic.
13. `.explore` on the activity's `find` Queries (today only the Nexus control uses exploration, `README.md:1243-1262`). Cost: medium.

Order: fn-126.5-8 as planned (they remove ~35 ceremony lines and give the structure lint), then P1 as one spec (≈ +80 lines, closes most of A's precision lead), then P2.
